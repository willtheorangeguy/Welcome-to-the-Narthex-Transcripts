• The significance of humiliation in Christ's life, including its connection to incarnation, death, resurrection, and ascension
• Christ's humiliation involves taking on human nature without losing divinity
• The concept of "condescending" or "humiliating" oneself refers to Jesus' willingness to take on human form
• The extra Calvinisticum is a theological term referring to the idea that Christ's divinity is not diminished in the incarnation
• This idea is rooted in biblical passages such as Colossians 1 and emphasizes Christ's ongoing supremacy and fullness of deity
• Calvin's concept of the extra Calvinisticum was developed in response to Lutheran theologians' questions about Christ's presence in the Lord's Supper
• Analogies, such as C.S. Lewis writing himself into a book, can help illustrate the idea that Jesus remains fully God despite taking on human form
• The concept of the extra Calvinisticum has been influential throughout church history and is still discussed today by theologians such as Gavin Ortlund
